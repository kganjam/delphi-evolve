# Critic attack: "how many proposed Turing circuits can reach the diffusion ratio their model requires?"

Sat 31 Oct 2026, ~06:50 PDT. Claude Sonnet 5.5. Answers the director/host request in `2026-10-31-POST-AUDIT-what-survives.md`. Search-level prior-art (several items from memory are flagged); I did not enumerate the literature, so no count of circuits is claimed.

## Verdict
**The mechanism is already well known, including for the exact circuit the idea would lead with. The *census* may be new; as a headline it is thin, data-poor and partly circular. Do not put "binding sets the effective diffusion ratio" or "Stokes–Einstein ratios are too small for Turing" on a slide as findings.**

## 1. How known is it? (very)
- **Differential diffusivity is the textbook requirement**, and its size depends on the kinetics (not a universal 10). Müller et al., *Science* 336:721 (2012), measured it in zebrafish: effective FRAP diffusivities Cyclops-GFP 0.7±0.2, Squint-GFP 3.2±0.5, Lefty1-GFP 11.1±0.6, Lefty2-GFP 18.9±3.0 µm²/s (via PMC3525670; tool-summarised, confirm before any slide); they cite models needing a clearance-normalised ratio ℜ>6, report a measured Lefty/Nodal average ℜ≈14, and say differential binding interactions and a ratio of at least 5–15-fold may be general. So **Nodal/Lefty — the example in the host's pitch — is the published demonstration that the ratio can be met, and by effective (not bare) diffusion.**
- **Binding/ECM adsorption as the source of an effective ratio** is established: classical effective-diffusion-with-binding theory (Crank; Lander and colleagues on whether morphogen gradients arise by diffusion — *from memory, unverified*), Nesterenko et al., PLoS ONE 12:e0171212 (2017; ECM adsorption as a realistic source of differential diffusion), Korvasová/Gaffney/Maini et al. (Oxford; a species binding reversibly to an immobile substrate relaxes the Turing constraints even with equal free diffusivities; assumes/relaxes fast-binding), and a tetrapod-limb modelling review noting differential diffusion is not necessary for most Turing models. **Turing patterning with equal diffusivities via network structure** (Marcon, Diego, Sharpe, Müller, eLife 2016 — *from memory, unverified*) means "needs Dv≫Du" is itself not a theorem about biological circuits.
- **Stokes–Einstein scaling** (D ∝ 1/R ∝ M^(−1/3) for globular proteins in dilute solution) is textbook. It implies mass ratios of ≈27, ≈216 and ≈1000 for diffusivity ratios 3, 6 and 10. It does not constrain small-molecule/protein pairs (a 0.3 kDa morphogen against a 30 kDa inhibitor gives ≈4.6 by this scaling, still < 6, but ionic/peptide cases and membrane-tethered vs diffusible species change the picture). In cytoplasm and extracellular matrix the size dependence is not the dilute-limit cube root (hindered diffusion; from memory).

## 2. What the idea does not survive
1. **The "required ratio" is model-specific.** Under an arbitrary prior I sampled (20,000 random activator–inhibitor Jacobians, `diffusion_ratio_context.json`), the critical Dv/Du had quantiles 5%: 4.0, 25%: 10.2, 50%: 25, 75%: 79, 95%: 464; 12% needed < 6 and 25% < 10. That supports "often large" but is entirely prior-dependent, and real circuits come with their own J. Any census needs each paper's own J; many models do not publish a measurable J.
2. **The numerator is mostly unmeasured.** Pairs with measured *effective* diffusivities for both partners in vivo are rare (Nodal/Lefty is the best-known; a handful of others exist, e.g. BMP/Noggin-type, FGF/Dpp-type systems — I did not enumerate). A census of "how many proposed circuits" with measured D would likely have N in single digits; the rest are `underdetermined`, which is the correct Scope Desk verdict, not a statistic.
3. **The BioNeMo/ESMFold bridge computes the part that is known not to decide the answer.** Structure → hydrodynamic radius → bare Stokes–Einstein ratio is a function of mass^(1/3) up to shape. The measured pairs show tissue effective ratios of 3.5–27 (Lefty1/Squint 3.47; Lefty2/Squint 5.91; Lefty1/Cyclops 15.9; Lefty2/Cyclops 27) driven by binding and interactions, not by size. So the pipeline's honest outcome is "bare ratio ≈1–3, as everyone expects; measured effective ratio differs by up to an order of magnitude". That is a real **validation test** (does bare S–E predict measured pairs?) with a probable negative answer, which is nice and fully honest, but it is not a discovery and it does not use a protein language/structure model for anything that mass alone does not give. If BioNeMo is used here it must be reported as no better than the mass^(1/3) baseline unless it is.
4. **Predicting binding-induced effective D from sequence** is the genuinely hard, interesting part and nobody has a validated method; do not imply the pipeline does it.

## 3. What can be done honestly in the time (small, fits the Scope Desk)
- Add a **diffusion-ratio scope family**: given a circuit's J (exact), the model's critical ratio d_c (exact, as in `fam_wavelength.critical_d`), and stated effective diffusivities with provenance → `applies` (ratio ≥ d_c with margin) / `out_of_scope` (ratio < d_c) / `underdetermined` (effective D unmeasured or binding unspecified). The Nodal/Lefty case is a real worked example where the verdict **flips with which Nodal/Lefty pair is chosen** (3.47 vs 27 against a requirement of the order of 6), which is a clean, sourced demonstration that a stated ratio is not a property of "the circuit".
- Use OpenResearch/literature mining only to collect **measured effective D with units, method (FRAP/FCS), tissue and provenance**; report the count of circuits with both partners measured, however small, together with a table of exclusions.
- Compare bare S–E (mass^(1/3)) with the measured pairs as the only validation the BioNeMo step can face; state the result whatever it is.

## 4. Slide rules
- Safe: "Turing needs a model-specific diffusivity ratio; measured effective ratios in a real morphogen pair range 3.5–27; whether a circuit meets its own requirement depends on which measured pair and on binding, which is why a scope check must name its inputs."
- Unsafe: "first", "we discovered binding sets effective diffusion", "BioNeMo shows why the textbook fails", any count of circuits we did not tabulate, any 'AI predicts diffusivity ratio' claim.
- Needed before claiming anything beyond that: direct reading of Müller 2012 (confirm numbers), the Oxford and Nesterenko papers, and verification of the two from-memory citations (Lander; Marcon 2016).
