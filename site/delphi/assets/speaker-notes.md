**Slide 1** (61 words)

Delphi turns candidate laws into checked theorems. The conditions behind these laws are known to experts, so we do not present them as discoveries. The problem is an agent that applies a law outside its scope without checking. We make that check executable, and we sort everything three ways: proved, simulated, observed. We ran no experiment. Anything unmeasured is marked pending.

**Slide 2** (70 words)

First check. Take a declared two-species model where the textbook continuum condition holds. On a finite domain only certain wavenumbers exist. At length pi none falls in the unstable band, so it is stable. At three halves pi and two pi a mode grows; at five halves pi it is stable again. Only pi and two pi are compiled in Lean. This is a linear statement; nonlinear simulation is pending.

Second check. The Price identity, a selection-only lemma, and a conditional Hamilton regression theorem depend on each other in the Lean source. Each assumption has an exact compiled witness showing what breaks without it, and two changed-sign versions are rejected by the compiler. Fisher's additive genetic theorem is not proved; it is pending. This is established mathematics, a regression statement, not a causal rule.

**Slide 4** (71 words)

Our own failure. We wrote a theorem assuming no mode has negative determinant and concluding every mode has non-negative determinant. A tautology. It compiled and passed the axiom audit. An independent reviewer caught it, not us. A revised version now compiles, with the determinant assumed and no mutants yet. Five more errors from this team were each caught by independent review. That is the check working, not a machine detecting alone.
